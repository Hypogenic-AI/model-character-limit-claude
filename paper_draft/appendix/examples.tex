\section{Example Story}
\label{app:example}

Below is a complete example from the 4-character condition, illustrating the three-phase story structure used in our experiments.

\para{Introduction phase.}
\begin{quote}
\small
Viktor was in the market, carrying a tattered map.
Diana was in the workshop, carrying a crystal vial.
Ivan was in the terrace, carrying a leather journal.
Suki was in the library, carrying a woven basket.
\end{quote}

\para{Event phase.}
\begin{quote}
\small
Diana set down a crystal vial. Diana picked up a golden key.
Viktor went to the courtyard.
Ivan headed over to the greenhouse.
Suki strolled into the forge.

Viktor made their way to the tavern.
Diana walked to the balcony.
Ivan put a leather journal aside. Ivan grabbed a silk ribbon.
Suki went to the attic.
\end{quote}

\para{Query and ground truth.}
\begin{quote}
\small
\textbf{Q:} Where is Viktor now? \textbf{A:} the tavern \\
\textbf{Q:} What is Viktor carrying? \textbf{A:} a tattered map \\
\textbf{Q:} Where is Diana now? \textbf{A:} the balcony \\
\textbf{Q:} What is Diana carrying? \textbf{A:} a golden key \\
\textbf{Q:} Where is Ivan now? \textbf{A:} the greenhouse \\
\textbf{Q:} What is Ivan carrying? \textbf{A:} a silk ribbon \\
\textbf{Q:} Where is Suki now? \textbf{A:} the attic \\
\textbf{Q:} What is Suki carrying? \textbf{A:} a woven basket
\end{quote}

Note that Viktor's possession (tattered map) and Suki's possession (woven basket) do not change during the story, while Diana's and Ivan's possessions are swapped via drop-then-pickup sequences.
The model must track all eight attribute values simultaneously and report them via structured JSON output.
